About

Built in Hyderabad.
Thinking from first principles.

Prexi is built by Yudi Labs — a small AI research team focused on making LLMs structurally better, not just faster.

01Origin

Why we built this.

Every AI product we built hit the same wall. By turn 10 of a conversation, you're sending thousands of tokens to the LLM — most of which is noise from earlier turns. The model's attention dilutes. Costs climb. Quality drops.

The existing solutions were either too broad (truncate everything after N tokens) or too opinionated (extract facts into a graph, then re-inject them). Neither approach preserved conversational meaning well. Neither knew when to leave a conversation alone.

Prexi started from a simple question: what does the brain do here? Humans don't replay full conversation transcripts to recall context. They compress — keeping signal, discarding noise. The key insight is that compression is domain-specific. You compress a casual chat aggressively. You compress a legal document not at all.

02Mission

What we're trying to do.

Make context management invisible infrastructure. The same way you don't think about TCP/IP when you send an email — you shouldn't have to think about context compression when you build an AI product.

The long-term goal: Prexi becomes the default layer between applications and LLMs — the thing that makes AI applications cheaper to run, better in quality, and safer by default.

03Principles

How we think.

First principles, not benchmarks

We start by asking what the brain does, then ask if that's what we should do. Benchmarks validate — they don't design.

Hard floors, not soft guardrails

Protected zones are architectural, not configurable. Some decisions should not be delegatable to end users or API callers.

Reproducibility is the claim

Every number we publish comes with a command you can run. If you can't verify it, we won't claim it.

Sparse, local, continual

Our broader research agenda at Yudi Labs. AI systems should be sparse in what they attend to, local in how they learn, and continual in how they improve. Prexi is one expression of these principles.
